This future pays Yes in 2042. Repeat 288,000 times.

To get a probability, I turned the one smooth line into thousands of realistic futures. First, start from today. The model doesn’t match recent temperatures exactly, so I anchored it to the observed 2006–2025 average and started every future from 2026’s actual data, about 1.52°C so far. Second, add noise. Real years bounce around the trend because of El Niño, La Niña and volcanoes. I estimated that from 1880–2025: each year keeps about half of the previous year’s swing and adds a new random shock of about a tenth of a degree. Third, vary the settings that matter most: climate sensitivity, how fast the climate responds, the emissions path, and the land-use emissions the class model leaves out. Fourth, score every future exactly like Kalshi: four datasets, each rounded to two decimals, and a year counts only when the second-highest is at least 2.00°C. The table is one real simulated future. In 2027 an El Niño year pushes it up, but only to 1.63. In 2041 one dataset alone reaches 1.98, which isn’t enough. In 2042 the trend plus a warm year gives a second-highest reading of 2.09, so this future pays Yes. Then I repeated that 288,000 times.
