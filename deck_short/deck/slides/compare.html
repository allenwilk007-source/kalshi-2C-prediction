3 · HOW WE COMPARE

Our numbers next to Kalshi’s

Same middle

Every version puts the first 2°C year around 2044, and the IPCC agrees on about 2°C for 2041–2060

Different width

A narrow PDF puts more futures past 2°C (81%); IPCC-sized uncertainty gives 67%, inside Kalshi’s range

Here are all the numbers side by side. The shaded gold band is the range Kalshi’s price allows once fees and the spread are counted, 65% to 73%. The class model exactly as taught says 99%. Fitted to history, 93%. Our 72-version estimate, 81%. Kalshi, 70%. Our IPCC-calibrated version, 67%, inside Kalshi’s band. With the widest uncertainty, 54%. Why do they differ so much? Not because they disagree about where warming is heading. Every version puts the most likely first 2°C year around 2044, and the IPCC’s own projection is about 2°C for 2041 to 2060. They differ in how sure they are, which is the width of the probability density function. The middle of every version sits just past 2°C. A narrow, confident distribution puts most of its futures past the line, so it gives a high probability. A wider one, like the IPCC-calibrated version, spills more futures below 2°C, so it gives 67%, which is essentially what the market says. So our honest conclusion: if you trust the class model’s confidence, the market looks too low. If you think the IPCC’s wider uncertainty is right, as most climate scientists would, the market looks fairly priced.
