The part I find most useful is that the model is customizable. In the Tool Dashboard you choose each assumption yourself, and the odds update in about a tenth of a second. The table shows what each choice does, changing one factor at a time from the IPCC-calibrated 67%. How sure you are about the climate matters most: with the widest uncertainty the chance falls to 54%, and if you trust the class model’s fit to history it rises to 93%. You can also set the climate sensitivity and response speed yourself with sliders. The emissions path comes next: 44% if emissions fall to net zero by 2070, 75% if they double by 2050. Adding back land-use emissions adds about 5 points; if 2049’s data arrives too late to count, you lose about 5. The El Niño carry-over from this year barely matters for the 2040s. On the right is the dashboard’s “What moves the odds most?” panel, which does this live from whatever settings you pick. Click any option and it switches to it. Every combination gives your own probability next to Kalshi’s, and a Yes, No or Pass call.
