Two versions
72 versions of the class model: 81%. With IPCC-sized uncertainty: 67%
This is where my numbers come from: a probability density function. For every simulated future I recorded one number, the hottest reading the contract would ever see between 2027 and 2049. The chart plots the density of those readings, in other words where all the futures land. The link to Kalshi is exact. A future pays Yes if and only if its hottest reading is at least 2.00°C, so the probability of Yes is simply the area under the curve to the right of the gold line. There are two curves because I ran two versions. The green one averages 72 versions of the class model and puts 81% of its futures past 2°C. The blue one is the same model with its uncertainty widened to match the IPCC’s ranges. Both peak around 2.1°C, so they agree on the middle, but the blue one is wider, so only 67% of its futures land past the line. The next slide shows exactly where those two numbers come from.
