This is the market we compare against: Kalshi’s contract KXWARMING-50, “Will the world pass 2°C over pre-industrial levels before 2050?”, captured on 18 November 2026. Kalshi doesn’t have a climate model. Its number is simply a price. A Yes contract pays one dollar if any single calendar year before 2050 is at least 2°C warmer than the 1850–1900 average, and the rules at the bottom of the page say at least two of four agencies must report it: NASA’s Goddard Institute, NOAA, Berkeley Earth and HadCRUT. Because the contract pays one dollar, its price in cents reads as a probability. Traders post offers to buy and sell; that morning the best offer to buy was 68 cents and the best offer to sell 72, so the market sat at about 70%. By the time of this screenshot it showed 71.7%. Prices move a little every day. Two cautions. Trading isn’t free: buying Yes costs 72 cents plus a fee of about 1.4 cents, so any belief from about 65% to 73% is consistent with the price. And the market is small, only about $21,700 traded since it opened, so a single order can move it, like the drop you can see in September. That’s all of Kalshi. The rest of the talk is how our model gets its own number.
