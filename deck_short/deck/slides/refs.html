These are the sources behind the project, in case you want to check anything. The market data are Kalshi’s: the KXWARMING-50 market page and rules, its public trading API for the order book and price history, and its fee schedule. U.S. Treasury yields were used to discount a payout in 2050. The temperatures are the four datasets Kalshi settles on: NASA’s GISTEMP, NOAA’s NOAAGlobalTemp, Berkeley Earth and the Met Office’s HadCRUT5, plus Copernicus ERA5 daily data to fill in the months of 2026 the agencies hadn’t published yet. Emissions come from Our World in Data, which is based on the Global Carbon Budget, and the Mauna Loa CO₂ record comes from NOAA. The model itself is the one we built in FECON 390, homework 2 through 5, with the Kaya identity, and the IPCC AR6 report for the range of climate sensitivity and the 2041–2060 warming ranges used in the cautious 67% estimate. The social cost of carbon analysis uses Richard Tol’s meta-analysis and database. The full list, with links, is in SOURCES.md in the GitHub repo.
