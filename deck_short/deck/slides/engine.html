Here’s how my project works, starting with the engine: the climate model we built in class, from HW2 to HW5, rebuilt in Python. It has four steps. One, the Kaya identity: population times GDP per person times energy per dollar times CO₂ per unit of energy, for rich, middle and poor countries, gives emissions. Two, a five-box carbon cycle: some CO₂ stays in the air forever, the rest decays over 363, 74, 17 or 2 years, which gives the CO₂ concentration. Three, forcing: 5.35 times the log of CO₂ over 275. Four, a two-box temperature model, air and ocean, turns forcing into warming above the 1850–1900 average, which is the baseline Kalshi uses. The table runs it with the homework’s settings. CO₂ rises from 429 ppm today to 526 ppm in 2049, and warming from 1.34 to 2.09°C. I checked the Python version against every year of the HW5 workbook and it matches to rounding error. But this gives one smooth line, and one line can’t give a probability. The next slide is how I turned it into thousands of possible futures.
