Wider uncertainty cuts both ways: the IPCC view gives more chance of a very early 2°C year and of 2.5°C, but less chance of 2°C by 2049.
Kalshi only asks one question: 2.00°C by 2049. But our simulations answer every version of it, so we can see where the two versions of our model agree and where they don’t. Left table: change the deadline. Almost nobody thinks a 2°C year comes before 2035: 2% in our ensemble, 4% in the IPCC-calibrated view. By 2040 it’s still only about one in six. The odds climb fast through the 2040s, because that’s when the long-run trend reaches 2°C and any El Niño year can push a single reading over. Right table: change the threshold. 1.8°C by 2049 is nearly certain in both views. 2.2°C is about a one-in-three chance, and 2.5°C is very unlikely in both. The interesting pattern is in the callout. The IPCC-calibrated view is wider, not lower. So it gives more chance to the extremes: a 2°C year before 2035, or a reading of 2.5°C. But it gives less chance to the middle-of-the-road outcome of reaching 2°C by 2049. That’s because the middle of both views sits just above 2°C. When you widen a distribution whose middle is just above a line, you push more of it below the line. This is the clearest way to see that the disagreement is about confidence, not direction.
