This is the personal version of the whole project. Pick the row that matches what you believe will happen, and read across: the chance of a 2°C year it implies, the hypothetical call, and what $100 would do. Start from the highlighted row. If you think the IPCC-calibrated view is right, the chance is 67%, inside the band the market price allows, so you’d pass: the price is fair to you. Now move up the table, to beliefs that make 2°C less likely. If you believe emissions fall to net zero by 2070, the chance drops to 44%. No costs 34.5 cents, and you think No happens 56% of the time, so you’d buy No. $100 buys 289 No contracts: you’d win $189 if no year reaches 2°C, lose $100 if one does, and expect about $63. The same logic gives Buy No if you think uncertainty is as wide as the full IPCC range, if emissions stay flat, or if 2049’s data comes too late to count. Move down the table, to beliefs that make 2°C more likely. Adding back land-use emissions gets you to 73%, still just inside the band, so still pass. If emissions double by 2050, or if you trust our 72-version class model, or the class model’s fit to history, you’d buy Yes: $100 buys 136 Yes contracts, worth +$36 if a 2°C year happens and −$100 if not. The expected gain grows the more confident you are: $2, $10, $26. Two lessons. First, the payoff shape doesn’t change, only which side you’re on and how often you expect to win. Second, every Yes bet risks $100 to make $36, so it only makes sense if you’re quite sure. The Decision Lab lets you combine beliefs, not just change one at a time.
