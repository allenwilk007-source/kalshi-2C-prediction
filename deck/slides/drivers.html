WHAT MOVES THE ODDS

One factor at a time, from the IPCC-calibrated 67%

Which assumptions actually matter? We started from the IPCC-calibrated forecast, 67%, and changed one factor at a time while keeping everything else fixed. Each bar shows how far that one factor can move the answer. The gold line is Kalshi’s 70%. Climate certainty is by far the biggest. With the full IPCC width the answer drops to 54%; trusting the class model’s fit to history raises it to 93%. So the single most important question isn’t about economics or policy, it’s how confident we are about the climate’s response. Emissions come second: from 44% if emissions fall to net zero by 2070, to 75% if they double by 2050. That’s a big range, but notice it’s smaller than you might expect. Most of the warming that matters before 2050 is already locked in by CO₂ that’s already in the air, because CO₂ stays there for centuries. Land-use emissions, which the class Kaya model leaves out, add about 5 points. Whether 2049 counts, because its data may arrive after the contract expires, is worth about 5 points the other way. And the El Niño carry-over from this year’s warm end barely matters for the 2040s: a hot year fades within a few years. This is why the deck keeps coming back to confidence.
