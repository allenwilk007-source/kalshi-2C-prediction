STRATEGIES B & C · HYPOTHETICAL

Softening the loss, and an arbitrage check

B · Hedge

70% in the 2°C bet, 30% in No on “CO₂ ≥ 445 ppm before 2030” (model 1% vs market 21%). It pays in the slow-warming worlds where the 2°C bet loses.

C · Arbitrage

Two Kalshi markets ask the same question (2026 record). Yes on one + No on the other always pays $1. Today it costs $1.019: no free money.

Strategy B adds a hedge. Kalshi also has a market on whether CO₂ at Mauna Loa reaches 445 ppm before 2030. Our CO₂ model gives that about 1%, but the market prices it around 21%, so buying No looks cheap. CO₂ that high would only happen with very fast emissions and warming, so this leg tends to lose only in worlds where the 2°C bet wins, and pays in the slow-warming worlds where it loses. Splitting $1,000 seventy-thirty, the bad case improves from losing $1,000 to losing $638, and the good case falls from +$362 to +$315. Strategy C is a true arbitrage check. Kalshi lists two separate contracts that both ask whether 2026 will be NASA’s record year. Buying Yes on one and No on the other pays exactly $1 whatever happens, so if the pair ever costs less than $1 after fees, it’s free money. Today it costs $1.019, so there is no arbitrage. It would take a move of about 2 cents. One risk: one of the two tickers says “27”, and if Kalshi treats it as a 2027 market they stop being the same bet.
