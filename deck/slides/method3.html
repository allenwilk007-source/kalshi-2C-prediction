From many futures to a probability and a PDF

Repeat step 2 for N futures, each with its own climate settings, economy and noise
Probability: P = futures with a Yes year ÷ N. With N = 100,000 the sampling error is about ±0.3 points
PDF: for each future take M = highest 2nd-highest reading, 2027–2049; f(m) is the density of M
Link: a future pays Yes exactly when M ≥ 2.00, so P(Yes) = area under f(m) right of 2.00

IPCC-calibrated view, 100,000 futures: highest contract reading 2027–2049.

Step three is how one future becomes a probability, and where the probability density function comes in. We repeat step two many times. Each future gets its own climate settings, its own economy and its own random noise. The probability is simply a count: the number of futures that have at least one Yes year, divided by the total. With 100,000 futures the standard error of that count is the square root of p times one minus p over N, about 0.15 points, so our numbers are accurate to roughly plus or minus 0.3 points. The PDF: for each future, we record the highest reading the contract would ever see between 2027 and 2049, meaning the highest second-highest dataset. Call it M. The probability density function of M shows where all the futures end up. Here it is for the IPCC-calibrated view: it peaks around 2.07°C and most futures land between about 1.7 and 2.4. The link between the two is exact. A future pays Yes if and only if its highest reading M is at least 2.00. So the probability of Yes is the area under the density to the right of the 2.00 line: 67% here. The other 33% of futures never reach 2°C before 2050. Why use a PDF and not just the count? Because it shows the shape. Two forecasts can have the same middle but different widths, and the width decides how much area falls past 2.00. That’s the whole story of our 81% versus 67%. A detail for accuracy: we always report the counted share. The curve is smoothed for display, and because readings are rounded to 0.01, the smoothing spreads the futures sitting at exactly 2.00 across both sides of the line. That’s why the smoothed area comes out at 66% while the count is 67.1%.
