  • 77% of the spread comes from the damage estimates
Our very first question this semester was how to predict the social cost of carbon, so we asked the class model directly. We added one extra gigatonne of carbon in 2027 and followed the extra warming all the way to 2300. We turned that warming into damages using the 14,000 estimates in the course spreadsheet from Tol’s database, then discounted the damages back to today using the net-present-value method from HW1. At a 1.5% pure rate of time preference, the model gives about $66 per tonne of carbon, or $18 per tonne of CO₂. Tol’s published estimates at the same discount rate have a median of $143. At 3% we get $34 against Tol’s $29, the same ballpark. The biggest lesson: about three quarters of the uncertainty comes from how costly warming is, the damage function, not from the climate science or the economics. Discounting choices matter a lot too.
