Where 81% comes from: 72 versions, equally weighted

Versions: sensitivity p2 ∈ {1.00, 1.15, 1.30} × speed p1 ∈ {0.020, 0.0256, 0.032} × emissions {HW5, falling, flat, rising} × land use {off, on} = 72
Each version: 4,000 futures with the class noise from the 2026 start; P(v) = share that reach 2°C
Our number: P = (P(1) + … + P(72)) ÷ 72 = 80.8%, from 288,000 futures
A choice to defend: every version gets equal weight; none is weighted by how well it fits history

Step five explains the 81%, which is our headline “model” number and the one behind the hypothetical Yes trade. Instead of a statistical posterior, this is a sensitivity ensemble, the way modellers often present results. We took the four inputs that matter most and gave each a low, central and high value around the class settings. Climate sensitivity p2: 1.00, 1.15 as in class, and 1.30. Adjustment speed p1: 0.020, 0.0256 as in class, and 0.032. Emissions after 2024: the HW5 economy, or falling, flat or rising paths. And whether to add back the roughly 1 billion tonnes of carbon a year of land-use emissions that the Kaya block leaves out. Three times three times four times two is 72 versions. Each version is simulated exactly as in steps two and three: 4,000 futures with the class noise from the 2026 start, and P of v is the share of those futures that reach 2°C. Our estimate is the plain average of the 72 probabilities, which is the same as pooling all 288,000 futures: 80.8%. The table on the right shows where it comes from. Each cell averages the eight emissions and land-use versions for one combination of p2 and p1. With low sensitivity and slow adjustment it’s 28%; with the class settings it’s 93%; with high values it’s essentially 100%. The average of the nine cells is 80.8%. The choice a careful reader should question is the equal weighting. These 72 versions are not weighted by how well they fit history or by the IPCC’s ranges, and the grid is centred on the class p2, which implies about 4.3°C of warming per doubling, at the high end. That’s exactly why we also built the IPCC-calibrated 67% in step four, and why the deck presents both numbers.
