TAKEAWAYS

What we learned

1

The class model works

It matches 150 years of data and the IPCC’s middle: about 2°C in the late 2040s

2

The gap is confidence

81% vs 70% comes from how narrow the model is; with IPCC uncertainty we get 67%

3

The market is fair but thin

Its price sits inside our IPCC range; one $1,344 order moved it 10 points

4

No easy money

Trades only pay if you trust the narrow model; spread capture makes ~$10 a year

Four things to take away. One: the course model is a good model. Once connected to real data it reproduces 150 years of observations, and its middle estimate, about 2°C in the late 2040s, matches the IPCC. Two: the disagreement with the market is about confidence, not direction. Our ensemble says 81%, the market 70%. But the IPCC-calibrated version of the same model says 67%, inside the market’s range. Three: the market looks fairly priced but it’s small. One modest order moved it by ten points, and it seems to use information like El Niño forecasts that our model doesn’t have. Four: there’s no easy money. Every trade we looked at only pays if you believe the narrow class-model view over the IPCC’s uncertainty. The only strategy that doesn’t depend on that, earning the bid–ask spread, makes about ten dollars a year here.
