DOES THE MARKET FOLLOW THE DATA?

Traders saw the hot year coming first

  • Kalshi’s “2026 hottest year” market has traded about 20× as much as the 2°C one
  • We re-ran our forecast for every day of 2026 using only that day’s data
  • The market hit ~80¢ by August; our data-only model caught up in November
  • Traders were likely using El Niño forecasts, not just past temperatures
To test whether the market reacts to data, we used Kalshi’s busier sister market, “Will 2026 be the hottest year ever?”, which has traded about 480,000 contracts. We rebuilt our 2026 forecast for every day of the year, using only the data that existed on that day. The black line is the market and the red line is our data-only model. The market priced a record year at around 80 cents by August, while our model stayed below 40% until the exceptionally warm first days of November arrived. Daily moves barely line up, and the market tends to move a few days first. Our reading: traders were using seasonal El Niño forecasts, which our model doesn’t have. That’s a useful lesson. A market can know things a model built only on past data can’t. That wraps up Part 1. Kalshi’s 70% is the price a crowd of traders agrees on. Once fees and the spread are counted it really means anywhere from about 65% to 73%, it rests on a thin market, and it seems to use information like El Niño forecasts.
