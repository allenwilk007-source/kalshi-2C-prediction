STEPS 2–3

Real data, noise, and two views of the climate

  • Year-to-year noise (El Niño): about ±0.1°C, back-tested on 2000–2025
  • Fitted to history: very sure how fast warming continues
  • IPCC-calibrated: same model, uncertainty widened to IPCC AR6 size
  • Both centre on ~2.0°C for 2041–2060, like the IPCC
Same middle, different width. That difference drives the whole talk.
The class model draws one smooth line, but real temperatures bounce around by about a tenth of a degree from year to year because of El Niño, La Niña and volcanoes. We fitted that noise to the gap between the model and the four datasets, and back-tested it: fitting only to 1999 and simulating 2000–2025, 92% of real years fell inside the model’s 90% band. Then uncertainty. We ran the model two ways. Fitted to history, it picks the climate settings that best reproduce 1880–2020 temperatures and trusts them completely. Because the class model only includes CO₂, that makes it very sure about the future warming rate. The IPCC-calibrated view uses the same model but deliberately trusts the fit less, widening the spread until 2041–2060 matches the IPCC AR6 range. The reason is that real warming also depends on aerosols and methane, which a CO₂-only model can’t see. The chart shows the key point: both views centre on about 2.0°C for 2041–2060, which is the IPCC’s own best estimate for its middle scenario. They differ only in width.
