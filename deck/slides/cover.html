Our model with IPCC-sized uncertainty

Our question: what is the chance that any single year before 2050 is at least 2°C warmer than pre-industrial times? Kalshi, a regulated prediction market, has a contract on exactly that, and people trade it. Its price works as a probability: 70 cents means the market thinks about 70%. We took the climate model we built in class (the Kaya identity, the carbon cycle and the temperature model from HW2 to HW5), rebuilt it in Python, connected it to real temperature data and the live Kalshi prices, and asked whether our model agrees with the market. Three numbers to keep in mind for the whole talk. Kalshi says 70%. Our model, run 72 different ways, says about 81%. The same model with uncertainty widened to match the IPCC says 67%, almost exactly the market. Explaining that gap is the story of this presentation, in three parts: how Kalshi gets its number, how we get ours, and what you could hypothetically do with them.
