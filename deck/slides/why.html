WHY THIS PROJECT

Why test a class model against a market?

THE MARKET

Crowd belief with money behind it

A price pools what many traders think, including things no model has, like El Niño forecasts

THE MODEL

Every assumption is visible

Each input is a number from class that we can change and test, from GDP growth to climate sensitivity

THE GAP

Disagreement is the lesson

Where the two differ shows which assumption drives the answer, and whether a trade makes sense

Why this contract? It settles on public temperature data, runs to 2050, and asks exactly what the HW5 model predicts: how warm the world gets as emissions build up.
Why do this at all? A prediction market and a climate model are two very different ways of forecasting the same thing, and each is strong where the other is weak. The market is a crowd with money on the line. Anyone who thinks the price is wrong can trade against it, so the price ends up reflecting everything traders know, including information our model doesn’t have, like seasonal El Niño forecasts. But a market never tells you why it believes what it believes. The model is the opposite. Every assumption is a number we chose in class: population, GDP growth, energy intensity, carbon intensity, the carbon cycle, climate sensitivity. We can change each one and see what happens. But a model only knows what we put in. So the interesting part is the gap. If the model and the market disagree, one of them is missing something, and by changing the model’s assumptions we can find out which assumption closes the gap. That tells us both how much to trust the market and whether a trade would make sense. This particular contract is a good test because it settles on public data from NASA, NOAA, Berkeley Earth and HadCRUT, it runs all the way to 2050, which is the horizon our course model is built for, and it asks exactly the question the HW5 model answers: how warm the world gets as CO₂ accumulates.
