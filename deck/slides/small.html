Small orders also pay more fee per contract: Kalshi rounds each order up to the cent.

We also tested the idea of small, constant trades, using Kalshi’s real price history since the contract listed in June 2025, with $10 a week. Method 1 buys at the asking price every week, like dollar-cost averaging. You’d have paid 80 cents on average, because the price was higher last year. If our 81% is right you just about break even; if the IPCC’s 67% is right you’re down $94; sold today you’d be down $98. Method 2 posts a limit order one cent above the best bid each week. Patient buyers pay no fee on this market and get a better price, about 76 cents, so it does better: +$36 or −$58. Method 3 is spread capture: quote both a buy and a sell price each week and earn the gap when both fill. It’s the only one that doesn’t depend on who’s right about the climate. It made about $12 in 17 months, assuming both sides fill, which is optimistic in a market this thin. Verdict: small regular bets only pay if our 81% is right. The only model-free edge is acting as a patient market maker, and here that’s worth about $10 a year.
