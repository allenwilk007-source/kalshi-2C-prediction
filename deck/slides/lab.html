Everything in this talk can be tried live. We built a Decision Lab that runs the class model directly in a web browser, using 400 versions of the climate and the economy, and recomputes a forecast in about a tenth of a second. There are two paths. Path A takes our recommendation: it shows our 81% against Kalshi’s 70%, explains why that means buy Yes, shows the IPCC-calibrated second opinion that says pass, and then lets you play out the future. Each play draws one of the simulated futures and reads the contract year by year, so you can watch whether the bet pays. Play 100 at once and you see the odds turn into money: roughly 80 wins out of 100 for our model. Path B lets you build your own forecast step by step: choose the emissions path, how sure you are about the climate, whether to add land-use emissions, the El Niño carry-over, and whether 2049 counts. You get your own probability against Kalshi’s and a Yes, No or Pass call with what you could win or lose. Then four tools: play the future, an odds explorer for any threshold and deadline, a chart of what moves the odds where you can click any option to switch to it, and a price slider that shows where your call flips if the market moves. Every number in the Lab matches our Python results to within a fraction of a point. As always, it’s hypothetical: nothing places a trade. That’s Part 3. The numbers only turn into a decision once you say what you believe, and the Lab lets anyone do that. Let me finish with what we learned.
