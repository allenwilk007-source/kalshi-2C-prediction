FROM FORECAST TO DECISION

When is a price worth trading?

Buy YES

Yes costs 72¢ + 1.4¢ fee = 73.4¢. Worth it only if you believe more than 73.4%

Buy NO

No costs 34.5¢ with the fee. Worth it only if you believe less than 65.5%

Pass

In between, the price is fair to you once trading costs are paid

Before any trade idea, here’s the rule that turns a forecast into a decision, and it explains why some of our estimates say trade and others say don’t. A Yes contract pays $1 if a 2°C year happens. Buying one costs the 72-cent ask plus Kalshi’s fee of about 1.4 cents, so 73.4 cents in total. If you believe the chance is more than 73.4%, each contract is worth more to you than it costs: buy Yes. A No contract pays $1 if it doesn’t happen. It costs 34.5 cents including the fee. That’s worth it only if you believe the chance of No is more than 34.5%, in other words the chance of Yes is less than 65.5%. Between 65.5% and 73.4% neither side is worth buying. The market’s 70% isn’t one number, it’s a band, because the bid–ask spread and the fee eat any small edge. Now place our estimates on the line. The IPCC-calibrated view, 67%, and Kalshi’s 70% are both inside the pass zone: on that view the market is fairly priced. Our 72-version model, 81%, and the history fit, 93%, are both in the buy-Yes zone. So whether there’s a trade at all depends entirely on which version of our model you trust.
