First we rebuilt the HW5 workbook in Python. Population, GDP per person, energy intensity and carbon intensity for rich, middle and poor countries give emissions. Emissions go into the five-box carbon cycle, which gives CO₂ in parts per million. Forcing is 5.35 times the log of concentration over 275, and the two-box model turns forcing into atmospheric and ocean temperature. We checked it against every year of all four HW5 sheets, and the largest difference is about ten to the minus sixteen: rounding error. So everything later in the talk really is the class model. On the HW5 template economy, where emissions keep climbing, the smooth model crosses 2°C in 2047 and reaches 4.6°C by 2100. The chart shows the model’s path against the four real datasets: it tracks 150 years of observations well. One thing we found: Parameter 2 implies about 4.3°C of warming per doubling of CO₂, at the high end of the IPCC range.
